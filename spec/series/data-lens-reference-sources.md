# Data Lens reference sources

Status: required inspiration/reference panel for Today’s Data Lens.

## Purpose

This file is the starting point for Data Lens candidate generation. It exists to keep the daily series rooted in strong data-visualisation thinking rather than drifting into generic trivia, consumer advice or a topical fact with only a thin data connection.

These sources are for **inspiration, critique, visual principles and candidate discovery**. They are not automatically the final factual authority for an edition. Any empirical claim, current statistic or historical fact used in the finished card must still be verified against a primary, official or otherwise authoritative source.

## Required daily reference pass

Before selecting a Data Lens topic:

1. Read this file from `main`.
2. Consult at least **three relevant sources** from the reference panel, using recent or evergreen material as appropriate.
3. Generate at least **five plausible candidates** across more than one lane (for example: chart craft, perception, statistical interpretation, communication, encoding, historical visualisation or analytical thinking).
4. At least **two candidates must be directly inspired by an idea, example or principle found in the reference panel**.
5. Prefer a **UK-relevant or broadly recognisable example** when it strengthens the lesson, but do not force geography at the expense of the visual/data insight.
6. Reject candidates that are mainly trivia, consumer advice or topical news unless the underlying lesson is genuinely about data, visualisation, metrics, communication or analytical reasoning.
7. Do not simply reproduce another author’s chart, wording or worked example. Extract the principle, then build a fresh deterministic example or use a separately verified real dataset.
8. Verify every factual claim in the chosen edition against primary, official or otherwise authoritative sources. Reference-panel sites can be cited when they are the original source of the design/perception principle itself.
9. Check recent Data Lens editions on `main` to avoid near-duplicates in topic, lesson or visual device.

## Selection test

A strong Data Lens candidate should pass all three questions:

- **Is the lesson genuinely about data, visualisation or analytical thinking?**
- **Can the reader understand the point in under a minute from one strong visual?**
- **Will the reader be able to notice, question or design something differently afterwards?**

If the answer to any of these is no, keep researching.

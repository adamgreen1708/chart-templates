Cornetto pubs. Starships. Impossible missions.

Simon Pegg's film career has spent two decades moving between very different worlds. Yet a fresh look at his IMDb ratings produces a surprisingly consistent pattern: his highest-rated eligible films keep returning to familiar company.

## The peaks keep familiar company

Start with the full picture. This snapshot contains **33 released movies** from Pegg's IMDb principal-actor credits, each with at least 1,000 votes.

The highest-rated titles repeatedly include the same three groups: *Mission: Impossible*, *Star Trek* and films directed by Edgar Wright.

That visual pattern becomes much clearer once the films are grouped.

## Familiar teams rate a point higher

The 12 films in those three recurring groups average **7.43** on IMDb.

The other 21 eligible movies average **6.09**.

That is a **1.34-point gap**.

The closeness of the three partnership averages is the interesting bit. *Star Trek* sits at **7.53**, Edgar Wright at **7.50**, and *Mission: Impossible* at **7.35**.

This is descriptive rather than causal. Two of the groups contain only three films, and an IMDb rating belongs to the title—not to Pegg's individual performance. Still, the pattern is hard to miss.

## Mission: Impossible is the long game

The film-by-film sequences add some texture.

Edgar Wright begins with *Shaun of the Dead* and *Hot Fuzz* at 7.8 before *The World's End* lands at 6.9. *Star Trek* steps from 7.9 to 7.7 to 7.0.

Then there is *Mission: Impossible*.

Pegg's six eligible *Mission: Impossible* films stretch from 2006 to 2025 and average **7.35**. After the first film at 6.9, the next four sit between 7.4 and 7.7 before *The Final Reckoning* lands at 7.1.

It is not a continuous trend line; it is six films across 19 years. But as a career thread, it is unusually durable.

## TLDR

Across 33 eligible Simon Pegg movies in this IMDb snapshot, the 12 films belonging to three recurring groups—*Mission: Impossible*, *Star Trek* and Edgar Wright—average **7.43**. The other 21 average **6.09**. Familiar company does not prove causation, but it is where the strongest title ratings keep clustering.

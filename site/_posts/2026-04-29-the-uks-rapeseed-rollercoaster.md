<p>Another quick sofa scroll, this visual and data story is prompted by an evening stroll (with Jeff the 🌭🐾) around the fields in beautiful Happisburgh (Norfolk, UK).</p>

<p>Whilst my own personal experience (and 🤧)  enjoys an Apr carpeted in yellow for yet another year, the data shows <em>A rise, a peak, and a wobble back down.</em></p>

<hr />

<h2><strong>The UK’s rapeseed boom peaked early</strong></h2>

<p>UK rapeseed production climbed for decades. Then it peaked.</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/the-uks-rapeseed-rollercoaster/img_7895.png" alt="IMG 7895" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Rape or colza seed &#8211; Production (tonnes) 1974-2024 (European Countries, <strong>UK</strong> highlight)</figcaption></figure>

<p>Since around 2011, the direction has flipped. While parts of Europe stayed higher, the UK drifted back.</p>

<hr />

<h2>And <strong>the drop came in swings</strong></h2>

<p>Zooming in, the decline hasn’t been steady.</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/the-uks-rapeseed-rollercoaster/img_7897.png" alt="IMG 7897" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Rape or colza seed Production (tonnes) 1974-2024, pct change year on year</figcaption></figure>

<p>Big swings year to year. And some sharp recent drops.</p>

<hr />

<h2>So what’s driving the swings?</h2>

<p>A mix of weather, pests, and incentives. Rapeseed is sensitive to conditions — wet autumns and dry springs have hit UK yields (AHDB). Pest pressure has also risen since neonicotinoid restrictions, with cabbage stem flea beetle a growing problem (DEFRA). Layer in shifting farm incentives post-CAP and Brexit, and it’s become a crop that’s harder to grow consistently.</p>

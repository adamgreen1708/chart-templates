I was watching *Fury* when Wardaddy dropped one of those lines that seems built to survive the film:

> “Ideals are peaceful. History is violent.”

That was enough to trigger a slightly unnecessary question: **where does Don “Wardaddy” Collier sit among Brad Pitt’s most quotable film roles?**

Not best performance. Not best film. Not most dialogue.

Just: which Pitt roles have accumulated the most quote entries on IMDb?

I started with the 54-film Brad Pitt list I used for an earlier post and checked his IMDb character page for each title. I could verify a quote-entry count for **44 film-role pages**. Ten remain unresolved, so they are excluded rather than quietly treated as zero.

## Fury started it. Tyler Durden owns the answer.

Wardaddy does pretty well.

He lands **8th**, with **40 IMDb quote entries**.

But Tyler Durden is on another level: **105**.

The numbers become more interesting when you add a little of the actual dialogue.

Tyler gets: “The things you own end up owning you.”

Billy Beane in *Moneyball*, third with 53 entries, gets the much warmer: “How can you not be romantic about baseball?”

And Sonny Hayes in *F1*, fifth with 46, offers the impressively reusable: “Hope is not a strategy.”

Very different characters. Very different films. All unusually easy to lift out of the scene and repeat later.

That does **not** mean those individual lines caused the counts. They simply make the ranking feel less abstract.

## Ratings help. They do not explain the quotes.

The obvious next question was whether highly rated films simply collect more quote entries.

There is something there.

Across the 44 verified film-role pages, quote count and IMDb rating have a **moderate positive relationship**: Pearson’s **r = 0.54**.

*Fury* sits almost in the middle of that story: **40 quote entries and a 7.6 IMDb rating**.

*Fight Club* is high on both measures: 105 quote entries and an 8.8 rating.

But *12 Years a Slave* sits at 8.1 with only **2** Pitt quote entries. *The Big Short* is 7.8 with **4**. Meanwhile *Sinbad: Legend of the Seven Seas* has **39** Pitt quote entries despite a 6.8 rating.

Role size matters. Fandom matters. How actively people add quotes to IMDb probably matters too.

So this is a relationship, not an explanation.

Wardaddy’s “Ideals are peaceful. History is violent.” is a good example of the sort of compact line that travels well outside its scene. But the chart cannot tell us *why* it travelled.

## Rusty did most of his talking on the first job

There is one comparison where we can remove at least one moving part: same actor, same character, same franchise.

Rusty Ryan appears across the Ocean’s trilogy.

His IMDb quote count goes:

- *Ocean’s Eleven* — **42**
- *Ocean’s Twelve* — **13**
- *Ocean’s Thirteen* — **17**

Which feels about right for the first film’s dry rhythm.

After Danny launches into one of his grand plans, Rusty simply asks:

> “Been practicing that speech, haven’t you?”

Forty-two entries in the first film. Thirteen in the second. Seventeen in the third.

Sometimes the first job really does get the best lines.

## TLDR

Watching *Fury* started the question, but Tyler Durden ran away with the data.

Among **44 verified Brad Pitt film-role pages**, *Fight Club* leads with **105 IMDb quote entries**. Wardaddy in *Fury* ranks **8th with 40**. Ratings and quote counts are moderately related, but supporting roles and several obvious exceptions show why rating alone cannot explain quotability.

And Rusty Ryan? Most of his IMDb quote entries arrived on the first heist.

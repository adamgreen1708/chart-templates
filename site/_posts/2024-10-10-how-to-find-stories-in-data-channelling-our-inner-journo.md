<h2>Finding Stories</h2>

<p>Data isn’t just a bunch of numbers—it’s full of stories waiting to be told. To find these stories, we need to think like journalists. Journalists dig deep, ask interesting questions, and reveal the narratives behind the facts. In the same way, we can explore our data thoughtfully and turn the insights into a story that connects with our audience.</p>

<h4>1. <strong>Start with a Question</strong></h4>

<p>Just like journalists start with a key question, it helps if we have one in mind when looking at our data. What are we trying to figure out? Maybe we’re curious about why a key performance metric is slipping or what’s driving the success of another. A clear question keeps us focused on the data that matters.</p>

<p>📢 <strong>Stay Curious</strong><br />Approaching data with curiosity opens up new possibilities. Our questions can guide us, and we can follow wherever the data leads. Often, the best insights come when we allow ourselves to explore.</p>

<h4>2. <strong>Find the Human Angle</strong></h4>

<p>Great stories connect with people, and our data can too. We can look for the human side behind the numbers. How does the data reflect real-life situations? What impact is it having on people?</p>

<p>For instance, instead of just noting that a particular performance metric is up, we might dig a little deeper. What’s causing it, and why? Maybe there’s a shift in process or a specific item that’s performing better.</p>

<p>📢<strong> Focus on the Human Story</strong></p>

<p>Every statistic has a story behind it. Our job is to find that story and make the data relatable. Connect the dots between the numbers and the real-world experiences they represent.</p>

<h4>3. <strong>Spot Patterns and Anomalies</strong></h4>

<p>With our question and human angle in mind, start diving into the data. Look for patterns that could reveal a story—trends over time, relationships between variables, or data points that stand out. These are the clues that will shape our narrative.</p>

<p>For instance, if you notice a sudden drop in a performance metric, don’t just record it—dig into why it’s happening. Is it linked to a recent process change or specific location? Could it be due to external factors like seasonality?</p>

<p>📢<strong> Dig Deeper</strong></p>

<p>Just like a journalist chasing a lead, dig into the details. Don’t stop at the surface—explore the underlying causes and connections. The deeper we go, the stronger our story will be.</p>

<h4>4. <strong>Verify our Findings</strong></h4>

<p>Like a journalist would fact-check their work, it’s important that we make sure our data is accurate before sharing our data driven stories. Double-check our sources and ensure our analysis is solid. This builds trust and helps avoid any misunderstandings.</p>

<p>📢<strong> Be Accurate</strong></p>

<p>Accuracy is everything. Check our data, validate our conclusions, and make sure our story is rock solid. Our credibility depends on it.</p>

<h4>5. <strong>Craft Your Narrative</strong></h4>

<p>Every good story has a clear beginning, middle, and end, and our data story should too. We can start by setting the stage with some context and our main question, guide our audience through the insights, and wrap up with what those findings mean and what should happen next.</p>

<p>A smooth flow helps people stay engaged and makes it easier to understand the story behind the data.</p>

<p>📢 <strong>Keep It Simple</strong><br />A clear, logical structure helps our audience follow along and makes even complex data easier to digest.</p>

<h4>6. <strong>Use Visuals to Support Your Story</strong></h4>

<p>Just like we consume our news stories and articles, a relevant photo or visual brings stories to life, we use visuals to make our data easier to understand. A well crafted chart can highlight key points and make our story more engaging.</p>

<p>Choose visuals that clearly support the narrative. Whether it’s a line chart showing growth or a bar chart highlighting category segments, the right visual can make our story more impactful.</p>

<p>📢<strong> Use Visuals Wisely</strong></p>

<p>Just like a journalist uses images to enhance a story, use charts and graphs to bring the data to life. Make sure each visual adds value to the narrative.</p>

<h4>7. <strong>Ask “So What?”</strong></h4>

<p>Journalists think about why their story matters. In data storytelling, we should do the same. What’s the significance of the findings? Why should our audience care? This question forces us to think beyond the data itself and focus on its impact.</p>

<p>📢<strong> Keep It Relevant</strong></p>

<p>Always connect our data story back to real-world implications. Our audience should walk away not just informed, but also understanding why our findings matter and what they can do about it.</p>

<h3>TL;DR</h3>

<p>Finding the story in data isn’t just about technical skills; it’s about thinking creatively. By starting with the right question, focusing on the human side, digging into patterns, making sure our findings are accurate, and crafting a clear narrative, we can turn raw data into a compelling story. Like a journalist uncovering the story behind the facts, we can reveal the insights behind the numbers and share a data driven story that drives action and understanding with our audience.</p>

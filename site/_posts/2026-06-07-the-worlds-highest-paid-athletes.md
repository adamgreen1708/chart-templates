<p>Imagine being one of the five highest-paid athletes on the planet — while barely collecting any of that money for actually playing.</p>

<p>That sounds impossible. Then you reach Shohei Ohtani.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.forbes.com/lists/athletes/">Forbes 2026 rankings</a> estimates that the world’s 50 highest-paid athletes collected a combined $4.1bn over the past year. Most of it still came from salaries, winnings and prize money.</p>

<p>But one athlete has flipped that model almost completely.</p>

<hr />

<h2>First, the giant at the top</h2>

<p>Cristiano Ronaldo remains the list’s financial superpower.</p>

<p>His estimated $300m total is $130m ahead of second-placed Canelo Álvarez. It is the sort of gap that makes an already extraordinary list look briefly ordinary.</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/the-worlds-highest-paid-athletes/img_8260.png" alt="img 8260" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Total (On Field and Off Field) Estimated Earnings</figcaption></figure>

<p>But total earnings only tell us how much. The more interesting question is where the money comes from.</p>

<hr />

<h2>The day job still matters</h2>

<p>Across the full top 50, around $3.0bn came from on-field earnings, compared with $1.1bn away from competition.</p>

<p>That means nearly three quarters of the money was still earned through salaries, winnings and prize money.</p>

<p>For all the talk of athletes becoming global brands, performance remains the main pay cheque for most of them.</p>

<p>Only six athletes earned more off the field than on it: Shohei Ohtani, LeBron James, Rory McIlroy, Carlos Alcaraz, Jannik Sinner and Stephen Curry.</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/the-worlds-highest-paid-athletes/img_8261.png" alt="img 8261" loading="lazy"><figcaption>On Field (horizontal) vs Off Field (vertical) earnings</figcaption></figure>

<p>Five sit reasonably close to the dividing line.</p>

<p>Ohtani is somewhere else entirely.</p>

<hr />

<h2>Then there is Ohtani</h2>

<p>Ohtani’s estimated earnings split is almost absurd: $2.6m on the field and $125m off it.</p>

<p>That off-field figure is $40m higher than LeBron James, the next biggest commercial earner in the dataset. It also accounts for roughly 98% of Ohtani’s total.</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/the-worlds-highest-paid-athletes/img_8263.png" alt="img 8263" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Off Field Earnings</figcaption></figure>

<p>There is an important contract detail behind the tiny on-field number: Ohtani’s heavily deferred Dodgers deal pushes most of his playing salary into future years.</p>

<p>So this is not a claim that his baseball talent is worth only $2.6m.</p>

<p>It does, however, reveal the scale of the commercial machine built around him right now. His sporting excellence created the fame. The fame is currently producing almost all of the visible income.</p>

<hr />

<h2>The real divide</h2>

<p>The modern athlete is often described as a brand. This list adds a useful reality check.</p>

<p>For most elite athletes, the sport still pays the bills. Brand power is a lucrative second engine rather than the whole vehicle.</p>

<p>Ohtani is the rare exception: a sporting superstar whose current earnings look more like a global entertainment franchise with a baseball career attached.</p>

<hr />

<h2>TLDR</h2>

<p>The top 50 earned an estimated $4.1bn, with roughly 73% coming on the field. Only six made more away from competition than through it. Shohei Ohtani stands alone: $125m off the field, $2.6m on it, and about 98% of his total generated beyond the diamond.</p>

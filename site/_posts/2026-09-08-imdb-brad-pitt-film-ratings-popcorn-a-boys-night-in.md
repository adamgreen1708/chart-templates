This post was prompted by a lovely weekend with the boy. The girls were away; the boys did parkrun, crazy golf, beach-front arcades, ice cream and a film night.

The film-night choice: **Ocean’s Eleven**. A solid 7.7 IMDb rating — above Pitt’s 7.1 career median in this list — and somehow a film released back in 2001.

Obviously my next move was to extract a list of 54 Brad Pitt films from IMDb for a quick coffeetableviz post. I expected an obvious ranking story. Best film. Worst film. Done.

But runtime, director and peak era were more interesting.

## Longer runtime tends to mean better rated

  <img src="{{ '/assets/migrated/brad-pitt/runtime.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Scatter plot of runtime against IMDb rating for 54 Brad Pitt films. Ratings generally rise with runtime, with Fight Club, Snatch, Babylon and Cutting Class highlighted." loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Films under 110 minutes average 6.09, while films running for 130 minutes or more average 7.59.</figcaption>
</figure>

| Runtime | Films | Average IMDb rating |
|---|---:|---:|
| Under 110 minutes | 14 | **6.09** |
| 110–129 minutes | 21 | **6.84** |
| 130–149 minutes | 11 | **7.62** |
| 150+ minutes | 8 | **7.56** |

Ratings generally rise with runtime in this 54-film list. That does not mean an extra half-hour magically improves a film. It does mean the higher-rated films tend to offer a few more Pitt screen minutes.

## Then there is Fincher

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/migrated/brad-pitt/directors.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Ranked dot plot of average IMDb rating for six repeat directors. David Fincher is highlighted at 8.40 across three films, ahead of Quentin Tarantino at 8.00." loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Among six directors with at least two films in the list, David Fincher ranks highest with an average IMDb rating of 8.40.</figcaption>
</figure>

Six directors appear at least twice. David Fincher comes out top: **8.40** across three films — *Seven* at 8.6, *Fight Club* at 8.8 and *The Curious Case of Benjamin Button* at 7.8.

Quentin Tarantino is next at 8.00 across two films. The pattern is not only about duration; some of Pitt’s strongest ratings also cluster around particular collaborators.

## The late 1990s were the sweet spot

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/migrated/brad-pitt/timeline.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Scatter timeline of 54 films by year and IMDb rating. Seven films from 1995 to 1999 are highlighted, with Fight Club at 8.8 and Cutting Class at 4.6 labelled. A reference line marks the list median of 7.1." loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Ratings span 4.2 points. The seven films from 1995–99 form the strongest rolling five-year run, averaging 7.63.</figcaption>
</figure>

Across the full list, ratings stretch from **4.6** for *Cutting Class* to **8.8** for *Fight Club*.

Compare every rolling five-year period containing at least three films and **1995–99** is the strongest run: seven films averaging **7.63**.

## TLDR

The story is less “which Brad Pitt film is best?” and more: **longer films tend to rate better, Fincher is his strongest repeat-director partnership in this list, and 1995–99 is the strongest five-year run**.

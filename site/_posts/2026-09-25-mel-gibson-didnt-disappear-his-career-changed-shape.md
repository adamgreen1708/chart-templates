For me, Mel Gibson was Martin Riggs first. *Lethal Weapon* was one of those films that seemed permanently attached to growing up. Then came *Braveheart*, and the career suddenly felt bigger than just being the bloke in front of the camera.

So what actually happened after that peak?

The data gives a better answer than “he disappeared”.

## The peak is where memory puts it

This IMDb snapshot contains **58 eligible acting films** with at least 1,000 votes.

Two of the obvious nostalgia anchors really do sit near the top: *Lethal Weapon* is rated **7.6**, while *Braveheart* reaches **8.3** — the highest-rated eligible acting title in this dataset.

The 1980s and 1990s also have stronger typical ratings than the recent run. The median eligible acting-film rating is **7.1** in the 1980s and **6.7** in the 1990s, compared with **5.6** for 2020–25.

That sounds like a decline story. Then the second chart gets in the way.

## He didn't stop acting. He got busier.

There is a real gap: no eligible acting films appear from **2004 to 2009**, with the next one arriving in 2010.

But after that, the volume comes back.

From 2020 to 2025 there are **16 eligible acting films**. The entire 1990s contains **13**. In 2022 alone, six eligible films appear.

So “where did he go?” is slightly the wrong question.

He came back — just into a very different shape of career: more films, lower title ratings on average, and a less consistent sense of being at the centre of the big mainstream run I remember.

## Directing became the second career

The other lane is much smaller.

Gibson has **six released directing features** in the analytical dataset, spread across more than three decades: *The Man Without a Face*, *Braveheart*, *The Passion of the Christ*, *Apocalypto*, *Hacksaw Ridge* and *Flight Risk*.

It is a sparse sequence, but it contains some striking points: *Braveheart* at **8.3**, *Apocalypto* at **7.9**, and *Hacksaw Ridge* at **8.1**.

The gaps matter too. Later directing releases are separated by stretches of **9, 10 and 9 years**. This was never a high-volume directing career; it was a second lane that appeared occasionally and sometimes landed very loudly.

## TLDR

The data does not really support a simple “Mel Gibson vanished” story.

There is a genuine acting gap, but what follows is actually a **busier** acting period — 16 eligible films in 2020–25 versus 13 in the 1990s — alongside lower title ratings. Meanwhile, directing becomes a small, irregular second career with several of his strongest-rated films.

Riggs made the movie star. *Braveheart* changed the job.

  <h2>Method note</h2>

The analytical dataset contains 62 titles: 58 acting rows and six directing rows, with two overlaps. IMDb ratings belong to each title, not to Gibson's individual performance or directing quality, and can change over time. Source snapshot acquired 25 September 2026. Academy milestones shown in the directing chart were checked against the Academy's official ceremony records.</p>

<h4>☕ Set up</h4>

<p>It was a clash of titans at SW19. World number one Jannik Sinner faced off against Carlos Alcaraz in the 2025 Wimbledon men’s final — the Spaniard chasing his third title, the Italian seeking his first. The visual above captures the ebb and flow of the match game by game, set by set, in a simple triangle plot (well that’s what I’m calling it).</p>

<h4>🎣 Hook</h4>

<p>Alcaraz took the first set with confident precision. So how did the match swing from Spanish flair to Italian history?</p>

<h4>📈 Rising insights</h4>

<p>The triangles map the winner of each game from 0–0 to set point, with green marking Sinner’s games and purple for Alcaraz. Early on, momentum sat with Carlos. The leftmost column shows him finishing the opener strong — Breaking Sinners serve twice!</p>

<p>But then the tide turns. In Sets 2, 3, and 4, something changes. Sinner’s green triangles stack from the bottom up, signalling that he repeatedly broke serve early and held steady. Each of those sets follows a similar 6–4 script, with the Italian getting the first break and never looking back.</p>

<h4>😮 Suspense</h4>

<p>Set 3 delivers a wobble, with both players holding firm, the triangle splits — but instead of a tug-of-war, Sinner strikes again. Alcaraz blinks, drops serve at 5-4, and Sinner doesn’t let him back in. Is it stamina? Tactics? A shift in belief?</p>

<h4>💡 Aha moment</h4>

<p>Look closer and you’ll spot the match’s subtle turning point: consistency. After the first set, Sinner barely puts a foot wrong on serve. He breaks early in sets 2 &amp; 4 (1–0, 2–1) and rides that cushion. Meanwhile, Alcaraz’s famed flair fades under pressure, his triangles thinning out just when he needs them most.</p>

<p>For me, who was gripped on the edge of my seat, Sinner didn’t just outplay — he outlasted, out-thought, and out-slid……..😆</p>

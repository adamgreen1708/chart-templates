I started with Bond villains expecting a fairly obvious age story: the baddies got younger as the franchise modernised.

They didn’t. Not really.

**Bond mostly caught up.**

## How old is a Bond villain?

Across 26 first-listed-villain portrayals from the 25 Eon films, ages at UK release run from **31.7 to 65.0**.

Lotte Lenya was almost 65 as Rosa Klebb in *From Russia with Love*. Will Yun Lee was 31.7 as Colonel Moon in *Die Another Day*.

## Bond used to fight his elders

Across the first seven Eon films, from 1962 to 1971, the first-listed villain averaged **14.3 years older than Bond**.

From 1973 onwards, the relationship flips: first-listed villains average **2.6 years younger**.

## Then Bond caught up

Connery’s first-listed villains averaged 13.8 years older than him. Brosnan’s averaged 7.6 years younger. Craig and his villains are almost exactly age-matched: just 0.4 years apart on average.

That is the better story. Bond villains did not simply get dramatically younger. Bond stopped being the young man walking into a room full of older enemies.

## Bonus Bond stat

**Le Chiffre got the best Bond film to be evil in.** Among the first-listed villains, *Casino Royale* has the highest IMDb rating in the 9 September 2026 snapshot at **8.0**, ahead of *Skyfall* at **7.8** and *Goldfinger* at **7.7**.

That is a film score, not a villain score — but still a strong piece of villain admin.

## TLDR

The age gap changed more than the villains did.

By the Craig era, Bond had finally grown into them.

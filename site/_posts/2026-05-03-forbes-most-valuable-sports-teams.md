<p>This post comes off the back of a dinner table debate with my boy: which global sports team is actually the <em>biggest</em> in the world?</p>

<p>As a huge basketball fan and full-on Stephen Curry worshipper 🛐, he was convinced the <strong>Golden State Warriors</strong> would be top of the pile.</p>

<p>Turns out… not quite.</p>

<p>According to Forbes’ 2025 valuations, the <strong>Dallas Cowboys</strong> sit comfortably at the top, and by some margin. The Warriors? Still right up there… just not number one.</p>

<hr />

<h2>We know the Cowboys are big. But is this the only story in this dataset?</h2>

<p>At first glance, this looks like a story about the <strong>#1</strong> ranked <em><strong>Dallas Cowboys</strong></em> &#8211; valued at a stratospheric <strong>$13 Billion </strong>😳 in the 2025 Forbes <strong>most</strong> <strong>valuable</strong> <strong>sports team</strong> rankings.</p>

<p>They’re the most valuable* (equity plus net debt &#8211; full definition at the bottom) team in the world. <em>Again</em> (#1 since 2016) thanks to unparalleled revenue generation, massive commercial sponsorship, and the ownership of the AT&amp;T stadium. Under Jerry Jones they function as THE premier business entity despite not winning the super bowel since 1996. </p>

<p>But once you start digging into the data, the story is much richer than first thought!</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/forbes-most-valuable-sports-teams/img_7958.png" alt="IMG 7958" loading="lazy"><figcaption><em>Cowboys vs the field (scatter: previous vs current value)</em></figcaption></figure>

<p>Yes, everyone’s going up. But Dallas isn’t just rising with the tide &#8211; they’re pulling away from it.</p>

<hr />

<h2>Zooming out…</h2>

<p>It’s not about the Cowboys anymore.</p>

<p>It’s about the <strong>NFL</strong>.</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/forbes-most-valuable-sports-teams/img_7960.png" alt="img 7960" loading="lazy"><figcaption><em>Ranked team values (NFL dominance)</em></figcaption></figure>

<p>Thirty of the top 50 teams are NFL franchises. NFL dominate the Forbes sport team rankings. This isn’t really a global leaderboard, it’s an NFL list with a few guests (baseball, basketball, motorsport and proper football).</p>

<hr />

<h2>So… does anything actually change?</h2>

<p>If values are rising across the board (Cowboys v’s the field) you’d expect a bit of re shuffling each year, however with the exception of a few teams rising or falling plus or minus ten places, the key story is football ⚽️ scoring a bit of a home goal 🥅 with Manchester, Liverpool and Barcelona all dropping ten or more places.</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/forbes-most-valuable-sports-teams/img_7962-1.png" alt="IMG 7962" loading="lazy"><figcaption><em>Rank movement (year-on-year change)</em></figcaption></figure>

<p>Given the structure is fairly fixed once you’re in position, you tend to stay there. Which makes these few big movers stand out even more.</p>

<hr />

<h2>Here’s where it gets interesting</h2>

<p>Because rankings didn’t change much… I looked at one year $ value growth. These values definitely do…</p>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/forbes-most-valuable-sports-teams/img_7963.png" alt="img 7963" loading="lazy"><figcaption><em>Value growth (who’s accelerating)</em></figcaption></figure>

<p>Almost everyone is going up. But some teams are accelerating, stretching the gap at the top, while others are just ticking along, which explains footballs ⚽️ home goal 🥅 story of relative stasis and NFL’s 🏈 accelerated growth 📈.</p>

<p>So the tide is rising… but it’s also pulling things further apart.</p>

<hr />

<h2>Shape of the Forbes top 50</h2>

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed"><img src="/assets/migrated/forbes-most-valuable-sports-teams/img_7965.png" alt="img 7965" loading="lazy"><figcaption><em>Value distribution by sport (different economic models)</em></figcaption></figure>

<p>Whilst some teams compete on the same pitch, they operate in completely different economic systems.</p>

<p>The NFL delivers consistency and scale. The NBA reaches the highest peaks. Global football appears more sporadically, and less predictably .</p>

<p>Different sports. Different models. Different outcomes.</p>

<hr />

<h2>TL;DR</h2>

<ul>
<li>The Cowboys aren’t just #1 — they’re pulling away</li>

<p>*Forbes definition (<em>Valuable</em>)</p>

<p>includes the economics of each team’s stadium—such as revenue from concerts and non-sporting events—but excludes the value of the actual stadium real estate. These annual valuations are driven by factors like media rights, sponsorship, and merchandise.</p>

<p><strong>Key Aspects of Forbes&#8217; Valuation Methodology:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Enterprise Value:</strong>&nbsp;Forbes defines value as the total worth of the team, including equity plus net debt.</li>

<li><strong>Stadium Economics:</strong>&nbsp;Valuations incorporate the revenue generated by the team&#8217;s stadium, including concerts and other non-sports events that go to the team&#8217;s owner, but&nbsp;<em>not</em>&nbsp;the value of the real estate itself.</li>

<li><strong>Revenue Streams:</strong>&nbsp;Key components include media rights (broadcast deals), sponsorship deals, and merchandise sales.</li>

<li><strong>Growth Drivers:</strong>&nbsp;Recent rankings have been dominated by NFL and NBA teams, largely driven by massive U.S. media deals and brand power.</li>

<li><strong>Team vs. Individual Focus:</strong>&nbsp;While soccer clubs like Real Madrid and Manchester United appear, U.S.-based leagues (NFL, NBA, MLB) frequently dominate the top 50 list due to higher franchise revenue sharing and developed commercial operations.</li>
</ul>

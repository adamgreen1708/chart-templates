Britain's most popular age? **35.**

There are a projected **982,419** people aged 35 in the UK in 2026. Most were born in 1990 or 1991, around the time the World Wide Web was starting to make itself known.

So much for everyone getting older. Apparently, we've got plenty of thirtysomethings too.

## 1. Every age has a backstory

Look along the population chart and you'll spot echoes of British history.

People aged around **77–78** were born near the NHS's launch in **1948**. Those in their early sixties arrived around the **1960s baby boom**.

And the people turning 30 this year? They were born around the year the Spice Girls released *Wannabe*.

Yes, that was **1996**. Sorry.

## 2. The balance is shifting

In **2026, 19.6%** of the UK is projected to be aged 65 or over. By **2050, that's 24.9%** — almost one in four.

Meanwhile, the share aged under 15 falls from **16.4% to 13.6%**.

Here's a way to feel those years passing: someone born in **1985**, the year Queen played Live Aid, turns **65 in 2050**.

Freddie Mercury probably didn't see that coming.

## 3. The biggest change is at the oldest ages

The UK's total population grows just **4.1%** in the projection from 2026 to 2050.

But the number aged **85 and over more than doubles**: from **1.80 million to 3.67 million**. That's **103.7% growth**.

For perspective, someone born when *Doctor Who* began in **1963** will turn **87 in 2050**.

No TARDIS required.

**The interesting bit:** Britain isn't just getting older. Its oldest age groups are growing much faster than the population overall.

That's worth remembering when we think about housing, health and care. Even if the soundtrack is excellent.

Whilst sat belly laughing at an episode of *Romesh Ranganathan’s Parents’ Evening*, inspiration for the next post began to build.

As a celebrity daughter and her entertainingly clueless — but confident — dad made hard work of identifying real villain names in a grid of spoof and bona fide 007 baddies, I started to create a rich custom dataset containing Bond villain names, actors, dates of birth, film titles, IMDb ratings, release dates and Bond actors from a selection of solid sources.

Keen to explore a less obvious three-chart blog, a story focused on the comparative age of Bond versus villain across the 007 film era felt like an entertaining twist.

## How old is a Bond villain?

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/migrated/007-villains-of-age/actor-age.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Ranked dot plot of 26 Bond-villain portrayals by actor age at UK release. Lotte Lenya as Rosa Klebb is oldest at almost 65; Will Yun Lee as Colonel Moon is youngest at 31.7." loading="lazy">
</figure>

Across 26 villains, ages run from **32 to 65**.

Lotte Lenya was almost 65 as Rosa Klebb in *From Russia with Love*. Will Yun Lee was almost 32 as Colonel Moon in *Die Another Day*.

## On Her Majesty’s Junior Service

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/migrated/007-villains-of-age/age-gap.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Dot plot of villain age minus Bond age for the Eon films. Early villains are mostly older than Bond; later films cluster nearer the same age or feature younger villains." loading="lazy">
</figure>

Across the first seven Eon films, from 1962 to 1971, villains averaged **14 years older than Bond**.

From 1973 onwards, the relationship flipped: villains averaged **two to three years younger**.

## Then Bond caught up

<figure class="story-chart full-bleed">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/migrated/007-villains-of-age/era-age.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Scatter plot comparing average Bond actor age with average villain actor age for each Bond era. Connery’s villains average 13.8 years older, Brosnan’s 7.6 years younger and Craig’s almost the same age." loading="lazy">
</figure>

Connery’s villains averaged 13.8 years older than him. Brosnan’s averaged 7.6 years younger. Craig and his villains are almost exactly age-matched: just 0.4 years apart on average.

The data suggests Bond did not simply face younger villains in later films; he aged into them. By the Craig era, Bond and his villains were almost exactly the same age. Is that interesting? I do not really know! But to the Bond enthusiast, it is quite a development in how the character is presented across the 007 franchise.

## TLDR

The age gap changed more than the villains did. James Bond and his primary adversaries evolved from the classic older antagonist to generational peers as the franchise’s tone shifted over the years.

By the Craig era, Bond had finally grown into them.

## Bond bonus stat

**Le Chiffre got the best Bond film to be evil in.** Among Bond villains, *Casino Royale* has the highest IMDb rating at **8.0**, ahead of *Skyfall* at **7.8** and *Goldfinger* at **7.7**.

A film score, not a villain score — but still a strong piece of villain admin.

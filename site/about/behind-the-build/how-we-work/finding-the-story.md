## The idea

My favourite data stories tend to be the ones I didn't set out to find.

A simple question leads to another, then another. Before long, I'm disappearing down an entirely different rabbit hole.

That's part of the enjoyment.

## The challenge

AI makes producing charts remarkably easy. But a ranking, an average and a trend don't necessarily tell us anything interesting.

We need to resist the temptation to start drawing too soon.

## How it works

Before creating charts, we explore several possible stories.

- What surprises us?
- What challenges our assumptions?
- What connections are hiding in the data?

We compare these possibilities, challenge the evidence and decide which discoveries are worth sharing.

## Under the bonnet

Our Robert Pattinson project began with an interest in his film career and connections to James Bond.

Exploring the ratings revealed an interesting contrast between the Twilight films and his other work.

Investigating the cast credits revealed something rather more unexpected.

Pierce Brosnan, a former James Bond, had already played Pattinson's father in Remember Me.

The story had become considerably more interesting than a simple analysis of film ratings.

## What didn't work

Our initial charts grouped films into broad career periods and counted connections to Bond films.

Both approaches concealed the interesting details.

We needed to look at individual films and identify the actual people involved, rather than simply count relationships.

We were summarising when we should have been investigating.

## What we learnt

We often structure our stories around three stages:

**Establish.** Give the reader something familiar.

**Complicate.** Challenge their expectations.

**Reveal.** Leave them with something worth remembering.

But three charts aren't compulsory. If there's only one genuinely interesting discovery, one excellent chart is enough.

Don't start with the chart.

Start with curiosity, follow the evidence and find something worth saying.

Then decide how best to show it.

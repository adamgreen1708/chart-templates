04 / Form follows data

Upload your numbers.

Choose a local CSV, or load public data directly from your GitHub repository. Maximum 2 MB and 10,000 input rows; the artwork shows up to 64 rows.

Local files stay on your device and are not uploaded to Coffeetableviz.

Or / Connect to GitHub

Paste a public CSV link from main or a specific Git commit. GitHub receives the request; no login or token is needed. Pinned commit links always load that exact snapshot.

After creating an artwork, selecting a category updates it immediately. For larger datasets, filter to keep the result meaningful.

05 / Compose the artwork

Same numbers. Different story.

Choose a designed poster, or keep experimenting with the original geometric modes. Real data determines the measurements; decoration never invents a value.

Poster Composer / Four art families

Four data-driven poster families. Geometry is calculated from your CSV; colour finishes stay separate.

Warm paper, an economical palette and delicate geometric linework. Gallery finishes override the standard palette swatches; tapping a palette swatch switches back to Studio Original. These artworks are original, not reproductions.

Choose a poster style to see its precise data-to-geometry mapping.

Mixed shapes follow the category when selected, otherwise repeat decoratively. Four palette colours repeat when there are more than four categories.

Form follows data. Pixel Art gives you squares to paint. Truchet gives you a rule to repeat. Bauhaus Art Studio adds circles, triangles, colour and — if you want — your own numbers. In Data Art mode, the solid shapes are measured rather than arbitrary: their filled areas represent the values when the zero-based proportional scale is selected. Load a local CSV or a public dataset from the Coffeetableviz GitHub repository on main. Larger CSVs can be explored with their first 64 rows; this limit is shown rather than hidden. Change the composition, shape family, colour rule and sorting, then make the headline, explanation and source line your own. The zero-based proportional area option preserves actual magnitude, while the optional contrast scale is explicitly labelled as min–max. Turn optional shape labels on to show the label, value, both, or displayed index; cramped labels are suppressed rather than colliding with their neighbours. Poster Composer adds proportional-area Data Mosaic, zero-based Typographic Broadside, measured Stripe Study and Circle Study, with separate composition rules instead of simply rearranging the same shapes. The Hidden Miles preset recreates a fixed 3 × 4 stepped diagonal with a cream cutout in the top-right of every tile. Choose its palette for red geometry on yellow; the individually editable Cutout shape works in all palettes. The rest is an exercise in creative freedom. Inspired by Running in Shapes and the original Hidden Miles Tableau artwork.

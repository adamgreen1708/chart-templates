Icons of Data.

The remarkable ways we turn information into understanding.

Not just charts. Maps, symbols, codes and even patterns you read with your fingertips. What do they have in common? Someone made a rather clever decision about how to represent information.

A line that disappears

In 1869 Charles Joseph Minard made the route of Napoleon's Russian campaign shrink as the army got smaller. One line, several jobs, and a rather uncomfortable history lesson.

Our reconstruction uses Minard's plotted estimates. You can also explore his original 1869 map, including its French labels and temperature trace.

The clever bit

How information becomes a mark

A chart isn't clever just because it looks different. The interesting part is what each shape, line or colour tells us. Minard is a good place to start.

01 / Position

Where?

Place the line on a map and you can follow the army's route.

02 / Width

How many?

Make the line thinner as the estimated number of men falls.

03 / Colour

Which way?

Pale marks show the advance. Dark marks show the retreat.

04 / Context

What else?

Add places, dates and temperatures to show more of the journey.

01 · A line that disappears

Minard · 1869

Position · Width · Colour · Context

Napoleon's 1812 invasion of Russia didn't end terribly well. In 1869, Charles Joseph Minard turned the story into a shrinking band across a map.

The line tells you where the army travelled. The width tells you how many men remained in his estimates.

One design decision. Rather a lot of history.

How information changes shape

The encoding library

Every icon makes choices about which property of a mark should carry information. These are six starting points. We'll keep expanding them.

01 · Where

Position

Where a line, symbol or dot sits can communicate location, sequence or quantity.

02 · How much

Width & area

A thicker band can mean more people. The area of a shape can carry a different kind of numerical comparison.

03 · Which kind

Colour

Contrast separates routes, categories or states. Colour can also communicate a numerical scale.

04 · What connects

Topology

For a transport network, the stations and how they connect may matter more than exact distances.

05 · What comes next

Sequence

Order can carry meaning even when the marks themselves are deliberately simple.

06 · What can be felt

Touch & patterns

Not all meaningful encodings are visual. Braille uses the position of raised dots in a tactile cell.

A few dates worth keeping

Specific milestones, not invention claims
  1. 1829Louis Braille publishes his description of the six-dot writing system; development began earlier.
  2. 1869Charles Joseph Minard publishes his figurative map of the 1812–1813 Russian campaign.
  3. 1933Harry Beck's diagrammatic Underground map is first issued; his design was conceived in 1931.

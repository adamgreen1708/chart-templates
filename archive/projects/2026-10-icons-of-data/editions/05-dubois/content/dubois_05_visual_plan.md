# Icons of Data #05 — Three-visual storyboard
**Object:** W. E. B. Du Bois, *City and Rural Population. 1890*, drawn c. 1900 for Paris Exposition, Library of Congress item 2013650430.

## Visual 01 — The original that coils a number
- Show the **genuine Library of Congress original plate** with accurate historical title/year, creator attribution, archive reference, image credit and rights note. No AI-generated archival substitute.
- Explain four population groups; most of the drawing is one red spiral labelled the rural-country-and-village group. Preserve page orientation and legible original labels; source has ageing/low-contrast text.
- The pictured population is **Black Georgia residents in 1890**, not the entire United States or Paris; chart prepared for display **circa 1900**.

## Visual 02 — Unwinding the numbers (modern comparison)
- Question: How large were the rural group and three urban-size groups relative to each other?
- **Do not render yet** until four exact numbers and original city-size boundaries are verified from high-resolution scan.
- Candidate transcription pending QA: 78,139 (cities >10,000), 8,025 (5,000–10,000), 37,699 (2,500–5,000), 734,952 (country and villages), total **858,815** from Census table 25. **These are provisional, not licensed final data**; conflicting online captions are recorded in source audit.
- Prefer **four horizontal bars starting at zero**, direct count labels, a single ordered scale to show magnitude; historical original colour categories if independently checked, with accessibility text. Largest first by house default. No invented unit transformations. Optional one annotation `~86% rural/villages` only if original numbers verified.
- Explain this is the **distribution** of 1890 categories, not a chronological trend. Geography and definition are scope constraints.
- All 4 categories must be exhaustive and non-overlapping as stated; if source categories uncertain, stop rather than manufacture a complete distribution.

## Visual 03 — Decode the Icon: why a spiral?
Four-panel analysis; intentionally **not** a second four-category bar chart.
1. **Space**: winding the biggest stroke into a coil reduces page footprint compared with one straight line (schematic only, no fake numerical reproduction).
2. **Contrast**: colour separates population groups, and the vast visual red presence creates emphasis; avoid guessing a universal semantics for the palette.
3. **Numbers matter**: close visual length comparisons are difficult across bends; exact printed labels restore quantitative precision.
4. **Boundaries matter**: show 1890 city-size thresholds; no direct mapping to today's urban/rural definitions or modern migration causes.
- Labels should explicitly distinguish **diagrammatic teaching examples** from the historical object. Use restrained Coffeetableviz palette, generous padding, clear mobile type and reasonable caption length.

## Editorial differentiation
01 = original artwork and provenance.
02 = precise statistical comparison after source verification.
03 = encoding and perceptual trade-off: spiral makes difference striking; more difficult to read precisely.

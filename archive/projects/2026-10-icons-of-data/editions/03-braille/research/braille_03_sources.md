## Matters for careful handling
- 'Six dots, 64 patterns' can be technically true including blank; but RNIB describes **63 raised-dot combinations**. Use precise wording.
- Colour must never imply the real tactile code depends on hue; use colour only as a visual teaching highlight.
- Images should convey the dot positions to sighted learners *and* provide equivalent written dot positions for screen readers; they can't simulate touch.
- Keep claims about innovation/freedom grounded in the documented reading/writing affordances of early braille, not generalized claims about all blind experiences.
- The blog's audience likes a dry tone, but jokes should target our visualisation assumptions, never blind readers.

# Dr Seuss read-aloud anatomy — research plan

## Status

Research / story discovery only.

Do not create chart configs until the book-level evidence and family-reading angle produce a story worth publishing.

## Core question

**What makes a Dr Seuss book so good to read aloud?**

This project starts from a real family-reading experience rather than from a generic author biography or sales story.

The working idea is that read-aloud appeal may come from several different things:

- manageable bedtime length;
- simple decoding for a child;
- repetition and predictability;
- rhyme and rhythm;
- tongue-twister / performance challenge;
- sound imitation and participation;
- narrative momentum;
- invented language and surprise.

The aim is to test which of these can be measured credibly and whether the books reveal a useful pattern.

## Objective measures

For each title, collect where available:

- line: Beginner Books / Bright & Early Books;
- first publication year;
- word count;
- Lexile measure;
- ATOS reading level;
- publisher-described read-aloud mechanic or emphasis.

Primary sources:

- Seussville / Penguin Random House for official collection scope, format and publisher descriptions;
- TeachingBooks for standardised word count, Lexile and ATOS values where available;
- Bookroo only where TeachingBooks did not expose a required metric.

Do not derive unique-word counts from unauthorised full-text copies.

## Important interpretation rule

Lexile and ATOS describe text complexity / independent reading difficulty. They are **not** direct measures of how much fun a parent has reading a book aloud.

That distinction is potentially the heart of the project.

## Personal layer — not yet collected

If the objective data shows enough structure, add a small family dataset rather than inventing an external popularity metric.

Potential user-supplied fields:

- how often the kids choose the book;
- how much Adam enjoys reading it aloud;
- how much the children join in / anticipate lines;
- optional: whether it feels like a quick, medium or full bedtime read.

These would be explicitly labelled family ratings, not universal quality scores.

# Publication package: Mel Gibson career pivot

## Editorial recommendation

Lead with the contradiction rather than a simple rise-and-fall story. The IMDb data validates the nostalgic peak around *Lethal Weapon* and *Braveheart*, then overturns the assumption that Gibson simply disappeared: after a genuine acting gap, the later career becomes busier in film count but lower-rated at title level, while directing develops as a much smaller, intermittent second lane.

## Published headline

**Mel Gibson didn't disappear. His career changed shape.**

## LinkedIn

Mel Gibson was Martin Riggs first for me.

Then *Braveheart* changed the job.

I went looking for a simple answer to “what happened next?” and the IMDb data made the story slightly less tidy.

There is a genuine acting gap: no eligible films from 2004–09.

But then the volume comes back.

- 13 eligible acting films in the 1990s
- 16 from 2020–25
- six in 2022 alone

The later titles are lower-rated on IMDb, though: median 5.6 for 2020–25 versus 6.7 in the 1990s.

At the same time, directing becomes a second, much sparser career: six released features from 1993 to 2025, including *Braveheart*, *Apocalypto* and *Hacksaw Ridge*.

So the answer is not really “he disappeared”.

The career changed shape.

## Feature-image metaphor

**One film career forks into two lanes.**

A single film strip represents the familiar movie-star career. At a central muted-red hinge, the path splits. The acting lane continues but contains a visible break before resuming with a denser run of frames, reflecting the real acting gap followed by higher later output. A second, thinner directing lane branches upward with only six widely spaced markers, reflecting the small, intermittent directing filmography.

The point is not “action star becomes director”. It is the approved editorial takeaway: the career changes shape rather than disappearing.

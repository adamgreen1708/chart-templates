# Story plan

## Recommended story route

Messi's Argentina story saved its most successful chapter until the end.

The three charts move from longevity and late-career workload, to the emotional/trophy reversal, to the counter-intuitive performance finding that his scoring rate accelerated late in the career.

## 3-chart sequence

### 1. 208 caps. Plenty left for the final act.
Role: Set the scene.

Annual Argentina appearances from 2005–2026. Messi made 208 senior appearances; 66 came from 2021 onwards, including a career-high 16 appearances in 2021. The chart marks the debut, the late-career period and the 2026 farewell.

### 2. Four finals lost. Then four won.
Role: Build the tension.

Nine senior finals reached by Messi. The first four ended in defeat from 2007–16. The next four — Copa América 2021, Finalissima 2022, World Cup 2022 and Copa América 2024 — all ended with trophies. The 2026 World Cup runner-up is kept visible as the final coda.

### 3. The scoring lift wasn’t a blip
Role: Land the aha.

Annual goals per Argentina appearance show the full journey rather than only a two-period summary. The 2012 peak provides earlier context; 2022 and 2026 show that high scoring output returned late in the career.

Reference averages:
- 2005–20: 71 goals in 142 appearances = 0.50 per appearance.
- 2021–26: 55 goals in 66 appearances = 0.83 per appearance.

The late period supplied 43.7% of Messi's Argentina goals from 31.7% of his appearances.

## Editorial guardrails

- Do not describe correlation as causation: winning trophies did not necessarily cause the scoring acceleration.
- Use senior Argentina A internationals only for caps/goals.
- Do not use AFA's internally inconsistent farewell total as the controlling count.
- Keep the 2026 World Cup runner-up visible; the ending was not a simple uninterrupted victory parade.
- Chart 1 owns appearance volume; Chart 3 owns scoring-rate detail. Do not overload Chart 1 with goals.

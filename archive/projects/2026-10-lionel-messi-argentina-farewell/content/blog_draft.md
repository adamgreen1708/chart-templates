Lionel Messi's Argentina career ended with a goal.

On 6 October 2026, his 208th and final senior international finished as a **3–0 win over Benin**. Messi scored once, supplied two assists and signed off with **126 international goals**.

It is an unusually tidy ending.

The career itself was rather less tidy.

For years, the Argentina version of the Messi story was dominated by what had not happened: no senior international trophy, four lost finals, a brief retirement after the 2016 Copa América.

Then the last chapter changed almost everything.

The useful question for a chart is not whether Messi was brilliant. We can probably leave that argument to the rest of the internet.

It is this:

**how much of his Argentina story actually happened near the end?**

## 208 caps. Plenty left for the final act.

Messi's senior Argentina career began in 2005 and stretched across **208 appearances**.

The surprising bit is how much activity remained late on.

From 2021 onwards he made **66 appearances** — nearly a third of his entire international career. In 2021 alone he played **16 times for Argentina**, his busiest calendar year.

That matters because it stops the final years looking like a ceremonial extension to an already-finished career.

They were busy.

And, as the next chart shows, they were rather consequential.

## Four finals lost. Then four won.

Before 2021, Messi had reached four senior finals with Argentina.

He lost all four:

**2007 Copa América → 2014 World Cup → 2015 Copa América → 2016 Copa América.**

After the fourth defeat he briefly retired from international football.

Then the sequence flipped.

**2021 Copa América → 2022 Finalissima → 2022 World Cup → 2024 Copa América.**

Four finals. Four trophies.

The 2026 World Cup final adds a useful wrinkle.

Argentina did not simply win forever after 2021. Messi's final tournament ended as a runner-up again.

But by then the shape of the career had changed.

If the story had stopped before 2021, the senior trophy count was zero.

By the end of 2024, it was four.

That is quite a late edit.

## The scoring lift wasn't a blip

The team success is the obvious change.

The less obvious one is Messi's own scoring rate.

Across **2005–20**, he scored **71 goals in 142 appearances**: exactly **0.50 goals per game**.

Across **2021–26**, he scored **55 in 66**: **0.83 per game**.

So the final six calendar years contained **43.7% of his Argentina goals from only 31.7% of his appearances**.

The annual view is more useful than two averages on their own, because it shows the late scoring lift in context.

There was an earlier peak.

In 2012 Messi scored **12 times in nine Argentina appearances**, a rate of **1.33 per game**.

But the later run is striking because it keeps reappearing. In 2022 he scored **18 in 14**. In 2023, **eight in eight**. Even in the final year, aged 39 for much of it, he finished with **11 goals in 12 appearances**.

That does not mean winning trophies caused the scoring increase.

It does mean the late Argentina years were doing two things at once: finally delivering the team success that had been missing, while also containing one of the most productive scoring stretches of Messi's international career.

## The ending changed the story

Twenty-one years is long enough for a reputation to harden.

For much of Messi's Argentina career, the story contained a stubborn asterisk: extraordinary player, no senior international trophy.

Then, very late, the asterisk disappeared.

The final six calendar years supplied **66 caps, 55 goals and four senior trophies**.

The career did not merely carry on.

It changed shape.

## TLDR

Lionel Messi finished his Argentina career with **208 appearances and 126 goals**.

From 2007 to 2016 he lost four senior finals. From 2021 to 2024 he won the next four. And over those final six calendar years his scoring rate rose from a pre-2021 average of **0.50 goals per appearance to 0.83**.

Sometimes the last chapter does more than finish the story.

It rewrites the summary.

Youth internationals and the 2008 Olympic tournament are excluded from the cap/goal totals. The finals chart includes the Finalissima as one of Messi's four senior Argentina trophies. AFA's farewell article contains inconsistent headline career totals, so those figures are not used as the controlling count.</p>

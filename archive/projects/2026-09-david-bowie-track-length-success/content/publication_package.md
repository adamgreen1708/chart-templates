# Publication package: David Bowie track length, style and success

## Editorial recommendation

Lead with the failed rule of thumb rather than another general Bowie-reinvention story.

Across 275 canonical studio-album tracks, song length changes substantially through the catalogue and across AllMusic's overlapping album Styles. But the success test is the useful turn: among 237 matched compositions, album-track duration has almost no linear relationship with current Spotify streams (Pearson r = 0.11), and 15 of the current top 20 matched studio compositions run at least four minutes.

The point is not that long songs succeed *because* they are long. The point is that Bowie repeatedly made long, structurally different album tracks without a simple length penalty showing up in the current catalogue.

## Blog excerpt

David Bowie's songs did not have one fixed shape. Across 275 canonical studio-album tracks, the typical length rises, falls and shifts with the catalogue's musical eras. The more surprising bit is what does *not* happen: shorter album tracks do not show a clear current-streaming advantage.

## LinkedIn

A lot of pop wisdom comes back to the same tidy number: three minutes.

David Bowie was not especially interested.

I rebuilt his lifetime solo studio catalogue at track level — **275 canonical album tracks across 26 albums** — then joined duration to AllMusic's album Styles, current Spotify streams and UK singles-chart history.

A few things stood out:

- the 1967 debut has a median track length of **2:49**
- *Station to Station* reaches **6:02**
- Blue-Eyed Soul-tagged albums have a **4:45** median track
- Glam Rock-tagged albums sit at **3:29**
- among 237 matched compositions, track length vs log current streams is only **r = 0.11**
- **15 of the current top 20** matched studio tracks run at least four minutes

That does not mean long songs cause success.

It means the simple “shorter wins” rule does not explain this catalogue very well.

## Method / comparability notes

- Track identity and duration come from the canonical MusicBrainz album spine.
- AllMusic Styles are **album-level**, overlapping classifications inherited by the tracks on those albums; they are not mutually exclusive song genres.
- Current popularity uses a dated Spotify/Kworb snapshot from **25 September 2026**.
- The stream analysis deduplicates matched versions to **237 compositions**.
- Historic success uses the main UK Official Singles Chart from Official Charts.
- Chart 3 compares canonical **album-track duration** with composition-level success. Some historical singles used shorter edits, so the chart does not claim the plotted album duration was always the exact version that charted.
- Downloads are not used as a whole-career success measure because the commercial download era covers only a small fraction of Bowie's career.
- The r = 0.11 result describes linear association between duration and **log10 current streams** within the matched catalogue; it is not a causal claim.

# Five-album findings: how representative is the hit?

## Final coverage

- Five albums
- 53 canonical album tracks
- 51/53 tracks with confident ReccoBeats Spotify-style audio-feature matches
- Full coverage: *Ziggy Stardust* 11/11, *Diamond Dogs* 11/11, *Hunky Dory* 11/11
- Partial coverage: *Heroes* 9/10, *Space Oddity* 9/10
- Missing feature rows: *Neuköln* and *(Don’t Sit Down)*
- Both missing rows retain their canonical duration and appear in the charts with blank feature marks

The final charts use a global tempo colour scale across all five albums, with Energy, Valence and Acousticness fixed to 0–1.

## Main editorial question

**Can one Bowie song tell you the whole album?**

The five albums were selected because they contain Bowie's five most-streamed canonical studio tracks in the 25 September 2026 Spotify/Kworb snapshot:

1. *Starman* — *Ziggy Stardust*
2. *“Heroes”* — *Heroes*
3. *Space Oddity* — *Space Oddity*
4. *Rebel Rebel* — *Diamond Dogs*
5. *Life on Mars?* — *Hunky Dory*

The red point in each chart lets the reader compare that entry-point song with the album around it.

## Cross-album contrasts

The five charts also show how different the albums themselves are:

- *Heroes* has the highest median Energy (**0.826**) and the lowest median Acousticness (**0.00994**) among the five.
- *Hunky Dory* has the lowest median Energy (**0.278**) and a much higher median Acousticness (**0.501**).
- *Diamond Dogs* has the highest median Acousticness (**0.624**) in this five-album set, even though *Rebel Rebel* itself is much lower at 0.209.
- *Space Oddity* has the highest median Tempo (**134.3 BPM**) and the widest duration spread, from 0:44 to 9:36.
- *Ziggy Stardust* is the most compact by median duration (**3:22**) and *Starman* sits unusually close to its median sound profile.

## Editorial conclusion

The useful story is not “the hit is always atypical”.

It is:

**A hit can be a surprisingly good guide to some parts of an album and a terrible guide to others.**

The five red dots work as entry points. The surrounding tracks show why an album needs more than one number — or one song — to describe it.

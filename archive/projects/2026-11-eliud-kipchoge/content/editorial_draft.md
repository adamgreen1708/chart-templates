# The record fell. The legacy didn't.

**Editorial review copy; no site publication yet.**

In April 2026, the marathon finally went under two hours in an official race.

**1:59:30.** Sabastian Sawe. London.

Not Eliud Kipchoge.

An odd opening to a Kipchoge story, perhaps. But records are there to be broken. Dominance is another matter.

A little dive into the **#ShortsInSports** marathon dataset, kindly shared by **Rob and Chris**, offers three rather good reasons to remember him.

## Eleven. Five. Quite a gap.

Across the six races in this dataset, Kipchoge won **11 times**. The next-best runners managed five.

![Ranked men’s marathon wins in the six-race dataset, with Kipchoge on 11, the next runners on five, and ISO3 nationality codes.](/assets/charts/eliud-kipchoge/01_eliud_majors_dominance.png){: .ctv-centred-chart }

Six of the seven leading names are Kenyan, with Ethiopia's Haile Gebrselassie the exception. But even in that company, Kipchoge is a long way out in front.

And his **two Olympic marathon golds** aren't included. Another couple of medals for the shelf.

## Fast at 29. Still winning at 38.

His first win here was **Chicago 2014**, aged 29. His last was **Berlin 2023**, aged 38.

And between those two? A pair of world records.

![Kipchoge’s 11 winning marathon times with faster times higher, city and age labels, and two Berlin world records highlighted in red.](/assets/charts/eliud-kipchoge/02_eliud_winning_times.png){: .ctv-centred-chart }

The red dots mark **Berlin 2018 (2:01:39)** and **Berlin 2022 (2:01:09)**. Four years apart, at ages 33 and 37.

The other times bounce around. Different courses, weather, tactics. Winning isn't necessarily about running a personal best every Sunday.

But setting your fastest official marathon time eight years after your first win here? That's some staying power.

## Seventy-eight seconds. In one go.

In 2018, Kipchoge took the men's world record from **2:02:57 to 2:01:39**.

A whopping **78 seconds**.

![Ten world record improvements after 2002, ranked by seconds saved: Kipchoge's Berlin 2018 78-second reduction leads Sawe's London 2026 65-second reduction.](/assets/charts/eliud-kipchoge/03_eliud_record_leap.png){: .ctv-centred-chart }

Since the 2002 baseline used here, no single improvement has beaten it — not even Sawe's extraordinary **65-second** leap in 2026.

There's another little Berlin story hiding in the labels: **eight of these ten record improvements** happened there.

Of course, Kipchoge had already gone under two hours in Vienna in 2019. His **1:59:40** was an organised challenge, not an official world record. Still quite an afternoon.

## TLDR

Sawe owns the official record. But Kipchoge's **11 major wins in this dataset** and **78-second record leap** remain remarkable.

The clock moved on. The legacy didn't.

### A note on the numbers

The starting point is the [**#ShortsInSports marathon majors dataset**](https://github.com/shortsinsports/Data/blob/4ea0ca9e403d706a734d41088ad63e571ab0ca21/SIS-marathon_major_winners_2000_2026.csv). **Thanks to Rob and Chris for providing the data.** The charts use the [Coffeetableviz working CSV](https://github.com/adamgreen1708/chart-templates/blob/2e784456670858668696c016fc5ea44ef9d4228d/data/marathon-majors/marathon_major_winners_men_2000_2026.csv), containing **149 completed men's races** across six city marathons from 2000–26. Berlin 2026 is absent, and Sydney isn't included. This is not a complete all-time Majors ranking.

Known name variants were reconciled; many source results haven't been independently checked. Chart 2 shows **winning performances only**, with race-day ages; Tokyo's 2021 edition was run in March 2022. Chart 3 compares the **ten official world-record improvements after the 2002 benchmark**, not all marathon performances.

Record figures were checked with [World Athletics (2018)](https://worldathletics.org/awards/news/berlin-marathon-2018-eliud-kipchoge-world-rec), [World Athletics (2022)](https://worldathletics.org/competitions/world-athletics-label-road-races/news/eliud-kipchoge-world-record-berlin-marathon-2022) and [World Athletics (2026)](https://worldathletics.org/news/press-releases/ratified-world-records-sawe-ehammer-charlton-mcrae-yan-duplantis-assefa).

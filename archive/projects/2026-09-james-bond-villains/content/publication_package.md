## Blog excerpt

Bond villains did not simply get younger. Across the early Eon films they were much older than Bond; by the Brosnan era they were younger, and by Craig the two sides were almost exactly age-matched.

## LinkedIn post

I thought Bond villains got younger.

The charts say something slightly different: Bond got older.

Across the first seven Eon films, the first-listed villain averaged 14.3 years older than Bond.

From 1973 onwards, they average 2.6 years younger.

By Bond era, the shift is even clearer:

- Connery: villains +13.8 years
- Brosnan: villains −7.6 years
- Craig: almost exactly the same age (+0.4)

The villain age range is wide — roughly 32 to 65 — but the bigger change is who Bond is standing opposite.

Bonus stat: among first-listed villains, Le Chiffre appears in the highest-rated Bond film in the IMDb snapshot: Casino Royale at 8.0.

Method: first-listed villain per film for consistency; ages calculated at official UK release dates. <em>Die Another Day</em> uses the mean age of its two portrayers for film-level comparisons. IMDb ratings are film-level context, not villain scores.

## Chart captions and alt text

### Chart 1 — Bond villains range from 32 to 65

**Caption:** Across 26 first-listed-villain portrayals from the 25 Eon films, ages at UK release range from 31.7 to 65.0. Lotte Lenya’s Rosa Klebb is the oldest portrayal in this view; Will Yun Lee’s Colonel Moon is the youngest.

**Alt text:** Ranked dot plot showing the age at UK release of actors portraying the first-listed villain identity across 25 Eon Bond films. There are 26 portrayal rows because Die Another Day uses two actors for Gustav Graves / Colonel Tan-Sun Moon. Ages range from 31.7 for Will Yun Lee to almost 65 for Lotte Lenya, with a median reference line at 43.5.

### Chart 2 — Bond used to fight his elders

**Caption:** Across the 1962–71 films, first-listed villains averaged 14.3 years older than Bond. From 1973 onwards, they averaged 2.6 years younger.

**Alt text:** Chronological dot plot of villain age minus Bond age for all 25 Eon films. Positive values mean the first-listed villain was older than Bond; negative values mean younger. The early films cluster above zero, Rosa Klebb is about 31.9 years older than Bond, and the Graves / Colonel Moon identity in Die Another Day is about 16.9 years younger than Bond.

### Chart 3 — Bond grew into his villains

**Caption:** Connery’s first-listed villains averaged 13.8 years older than him; Brosnan’s averaged 7.6 years younger; Craig and his villains are almost exactly age-matched.

**Alt text:** Scatter plot comparing average Bond age with average first-listed-villain age across the six Bond actor eras. A diagonal line marks equal ages. Connery and Lazenby sit well above the line, Moore and Dalton just below it, Brosnan further below it, and Craig almost exactly on the line.

### Chart 4 — Le Chiffre got the best Bond film to be evil in

**Caption:** In the IMDb snapshot dated 9 September 2026, Casino Royale is the highest-rated Eon Bond film at 8.0 among the 25 first-listed-villain rows, ahead of Skyfall at 7.8 and Goldfinger at 7.7. These are film ratings, not villain ratings.

**Alt text:** Ranked dot plot of IMDb film ratings for all 25 Eon Bond films, labelled by each film’s first-listed villain and film title. Le Chiffre in Casino Royale is highlighted at 8.0. Raoul Silva in Skyfall is second at 7.8, Auric Goldfinger in Goldfinger third at 7.7, and Graves / Colonel Moon in Die Another Day is lowest at 6.1.

# The season unfolded — Premier League 2025–26

**Category:** Sport  
**Status:** draft editorial copy, not a published post.

Arsenal won the league. That part of the story fits neatly into one line of a final table.

But a season rarely unfolds quite so tidily.

For 2025–26, all 380 Premier League results have been turned into twenty paths: one per club, stepping through the points earned in its first, second and eventually thirty-eighth league match.

The finish is familiar. Arsenal closed on **85 points**, Manchester City on **78**. Behind them, Manchester United finished third on **71**, while Tottenham ended seventeenth on **41**, two points above West Ham in eighteenth. Those numbers say where the teams ended up — not how straightforward, or otherwise, the trip felt.

That's what the interactive is for.

Choose a club, then slide through its season. Each point carries a real match: opponents, score, date, win, draw or defeat, and the league points accumulated by that stage. Select a second club if you want to put two journeys side by side. Or clear the selection and let the tangle of twenty lines speak for itself.

The original 2020–21 Tableau experiment was about watching the season unfold. This is a new version built for the Coffeetableviz website, with touch-friendly selection and the details just a tap away.

### A note on the data

The data cover all **380** Premier League matches from **15 August 2025 to 24 May 2026**. Results come from [Football-Data.co.uk](https://www.football-data.co.uk/mmz4281/2526/E0.csv) via an archived source snapshot, with finishing totals checked against the [official Premier League table](https://www.premierleague.com/en/tables/premier-league/2025-26).

The horizontal axis is each **club's own match number (1–38)**, not a shared calendar week. That's deliberate: clubs don't always play fixtures in the same order, especially when matches are rearranged. These lines compare points after equal numbers of games, not actual historical positions on the same date.

**TL;DR:** Twenty clubs, 380 matches and 38 steps each. The final league table gives the ending. The interactive lets you explore how every club got there.

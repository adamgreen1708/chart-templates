# Three-chart redesign — editorial review v2

**Date:** 7 November 2026
**Status:** Alternative design under review; not approved, not published.
**Original:** /2025/01/17/shades-of-heroism/ and Tim Leong's *Super Graphic* (2013).

## Why the original three-chart set was rejected
- The six-hue difference bars reduced an interesting, multicolour costume story to "red for heroes, green for villains".
- Four publisher bars drew more confidence than is justified from six purposively chosen people per group.
- Seven exception dots carried no meaningful quantitative axis; their colour and character stories were absent.

## Reframed argument
**Costumes are a visual shorthand, not a moral ID card.** The lead hue and its supporting colours tell a richer story than a single red/green statistic. The *same* leading colour can belong to a hero or a villain.

This is **descriptive editorial analysis of 24 curated character costume versions**, not a representative population sample or evidence of precisely measured garment colour areas.

## Redesigned chart flow
1. **Who wore it better?** `chart_v2_01_costume_gallery.csv` — 12 familiar costumes in a heroes/villains gallery. Order-specific **main / second / accent** colour chips deliberately resemble your original Tableau fingerprints. None of the chip sizes encode fabric proportions. Six examples per role; balanced publishers in the gallery (three each per role).
2. **Who let villains wear primaries?** `chart_v2_02_role_scorecard.csv` — show all 24 chosen characters as individual dominant-hue tiles, grouped by publisher and role. **8 of 12 heroes** versus **3 of 12 villains** have a red, blue or yellow main hue; underline the strict traditional RYB primary tiles. The 4 publisher × role subgroups remain visible (DC 3/6 heroes; Marvel 5/6 heroes; DC 1/6 villains; Marvel 2/6 villains), but no general publisher inference is made.
3. **Same colour. Opposite morals.** `chart_v2_03_colour_twins.csv` — the true payoff: five hero–villain pairs with the same coded main hue but different characters and supporting hues. Red: Flash/Magneto; blue: Superman/Thanos; yellow: Wolverine/Sinestro; green: Green Lantern/Green Goblin; black: Black Panther/Venom. These are editorially selected comparisons, **not all possible matches**.

## Data contract and visual integrity
- Chart configs use the **same repo** `src/render_538.py`, `render_profile='social_square'`, 1600px square, `#F3F4F6` background and house header/footer.
- The new reusable `palette_story` editorial chart type lives in `src/editorial_palette.py`, dispatched by `src/editorial_schematics.py`; variants: `gallery`, `scorecard`, `twins`. All row values and colors are read from the CSV generated by `scripts/build_chart_views.py`.
- The hue chips are **ordered categorical labels** (main, secondary, accent), never a pseudo-percentage stacked bar. Secondary colours include gold, silver, black, grey and white without falsely recoding them as traditional primaries.
- Role labels use the house blue/red contrast. Swatches use semantically meaningful hue samples, not arbitrary template highlights.
- A full-body comic-art source review of all 24 rows remains **incomplete**; dataset is explicitly provisional. Do not call the colour assignments independently measured or verified.
- Phone-size QA at 390px and 320px is mandatory before author review.

## Publication position
Do not replace the originally rendered chart assets or publish any Jekyll post. Preserve v1 and v2 separately for comparison. Never merge directly to main; publication requires explicit editorial approval, fixed costume references, a separate site PR and successful Pages deployment.

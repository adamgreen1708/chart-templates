# Pixel Universe: can you recognise a superhero in 256 squares?

There’s a lot you can do with 256 squares.

A slightly wonky pixel cat. An alarming number of frogs. Or, as it turns out, a small cast of comic-book characters with very familiar wardrobes.

A little while ago, [*Who wears the hero colours?*](https://coffeetableviz.com/stories/who-wears-the-hero-colours/) looked at an old comic-book idea: heroes tend to wear red, yellow and blue; villains have a soft spot for green and purple. The 24 hand-picked costumes turned up plenty of exceptions.

But the colours got me wondering about something else.

**What happens when you strip a character down to a tiny grid?**

## Six heroes. Very little room for capes.

Six heroes became six original 16×16 pixel portraits.

Superman gets blue, red and yellow. The Flash gets red and yellow. Batman keeps his grey and black, and Wonder Woman's red and blue have a little gold for company.

The figures aren't traced from comic panels. They’re simple little interpretations built around the colour labels from the original study.

At this size, there's not much room for fine detail. A little colour, a silhouette and a couple of well-placed contrasting squares have to do the work.

Whether they're instantly recognisable is something for the reader to decide.

## Meanwhile, in the green-and-purple corner…

The villains give the same small grid a different problem.

The Joker, Riddler, Lex Luthor and Green Goblin all have green and purple somewhere in their recorded palettes. Yet giving every one of them the same two-colour block would be a rather dull gallery.

So the shapes matter too: a hat, a sharp collar, a smirk, an angular mask.

Magneto brings red into the group. Venom goes almost completely black and white. It's a nice reminder that villains don't all have to shop in the same aisle.

## The fun bit? Make one your own.

The drawings aren't just pictures.

Each has an exact **256-cell design code**, which means the same little character can open inside the [Pixel Art Studio](https://coffeetableviz.com/resources/8x8-pixel-art-builder/).

The new [Pixel Universe gallery](https://coffeetableviz.com/resources/pixel-universe/) has twelve designs to choose from. Pick one, change the colours, improve the face, add a wonderfully unnecessary hat, then save or share your version.

Nobody is checking whether your Batman is the right shade of grey.

## A note on the data

The original superhero study contains 24 deliberately selected character costumes. This creative follow-up uses **twelve of those characters**, six heroes and six villains.

The main, supporting and accent colours are **qualitative editorial classifications**, not measurements of costume area. The extra face colours, outlines and individual pixel placements are artistic decisions. This is not a formal test of whether people can recognise characters.

These are original, unofficial fan-inspired pixel portraits, not reproduced comic art or artwork affiliated with Marvel or DC.

## TLDR

It started with a question about the colours heroes and villains wear.

Now there are twelve tiny portraits and a playground where you can pull them apart and put them back together.

**Sometimes a few colours and 256 squares make a pretty good starting point.**

# Priority editorial briefs — November 2026 to January 2027

These are **uncommissioned** article concepts. They contain no new analytical findings. Source details and anniversaries are in `data/promotion/editorial_radar_events.csv` and the monthly tables. The normal Coffeetableviz dataset → story discovery → three-chart plan → render/QA → copy/approval workflow applies.

## 1. 18 November 2026 — 35 years of U2's *Achtung Baby*

**Working hook:** "The reinvention happened between two records. Can the numbers catch it?"

**Real question:** What demonstrably changed across U2's surrounding *original studio albums*? Is the difference visible in track lengths, track counts, credits or gaps between releases? A subjective "sound reset" would need a reproducible method and a clear caveat.

**Data acquisition:** U2's official album tracklists and credits; use verified track durations/releases from authoritative music metadata, checking versions, reissues and live cuts. Album selection must be explicit; do not conflate *Rattle and Hum*'s live/studio material with an ordinary studio release.

**Three-chart *hypotheses to test***:
1. Release chronology and release gaps across a consistent album scope.
2. Track-length distributions before, during and after 1991.
3. Songwriting/producer credit patterns (only if complete comparable credits exist).

**Blockbuster connection:** None required. An eventual relevant film-related quote would be separate research, not a fabricated link. An album anniversary is sufficient for a strong article/social hook.

**Gate:** Confirm that source and scope support more than a decorative chronology. **Priority A; new article.**

## 2. 16 November 2026 — 25 years of the first *Harry Potter* film

**Working hook:** "Eight films. One franchise. Did the shape of the story change?"

**Real question:** Across the eight main films, how did runtimes, release intervals and IMDb ratings evolve? These are descriptive series, not proof that any creative decision caused a rating.

**Data acquisition:** IMDb official non-commercial title.basics and title.ratings, with explicit eight-film `tconst` list verified against official filmography. Keep one runtime definition per film and a labelled ratings snapshot; vote counts accompany averages.

**Three-chart *hypotheses to test***:
1. Eight-film release chronology and gaps.
2. Film runtimes as dots/bars on an honest consistent zero baseline where bars are used.
3. IMDb average ratings **alongside vote counts**, with exact snapshot date.

**Blockbuster connection:** The film's official 2026 anniversary statement cites 16 November 2001, but the quote's **exact theatrical event type and territory** still need two-source verification before using it as a locked RUN_DATE connection. The article does not depend on getting a quote approved.

**Gate:** Decide if the analysis adds genuine insight rather than eight points of trivia. **Priority A; new article.**

## 3. 24 November 2026 — 35 years since Freddie Mercury's death

**Working hook:** "Queen's catalogue wasn't all written by one voice."

**Real question:** How did credited songwriting spread across verified Queen studio albums, and did album track structures change? This is a remembrance, not a sensational comparison of illness, death or revenue.

**Data acquisition:** Official Queen album/track and writer credits, checking co-written and band-credit rules before making any percentage chart. Chart percentages require a declared denominator.

**Three-chart *hypotheses to test***:
1. Original album releases / time between albums.
2. Attributed writer-credit distribution by a clearly defined period.
3. Track-length distribution by period, if complete original-edition metadata is available.

**Blockbuster connection:** Film quotation research may be possible around *Bohemian Rhapsody*, but **must not** assume the 24 November date matches that film. No forced quote.

**Gate:** Respectful tone and genuinely valid song-credit allocation. **Priority A as an option; only commission if data quality warrants it.**

## 4. 19–20 December 2026 — Two film anniversaries, one decision

**Option A — *The Fellowship of the Ring*, 25 years on 19 December (US release)**

Question: does the original trilogy's IMDb response differ as much as its runtime and calendar spacing? Use a precise original theatrical-film scope. **Separate the London premiere, US general release and UK public release dates**; do not merge them.

Possible charts: release timeline; runtime comparison; rating dots with labelled vote counts. This may be too little data for three full charts, in which case a single stronger chart is better than padding.

**Option B — *Scream*, 30 years on 20 December (director's official release date)**

Question: how has the franchise changed from film to film? Use official IMDb feature-film records and an explicit cutoff date for any 2026 instalments; define continuity and exclusions before charting.

Possible charts: series timeline and sequel gaps; runtime distribution; IMDb ratings and votes.

**Editorial recommendation:** Commission **one**, not both, for the same weekend. *Scream* might provide a stronger film-by-film story, but the recommendation must be tested against available real data. The other can be a Blockbuster or short LinkedIn-only commemoration.

**Blockbuster connection:** Check exact release territory/date independently and find an exact verifiable spoken quote/speaker. Existing `Scream` Blockbuster from 3 October (Neve Campbell's birthday) is a historical source for creative inspiration, **not** the December date's evidence.

## 5. 8–14 January 2027 — Bowie's birthday and *Low* at 50

**Working umbrella:** "How to measure someone who kept changing."

**Occasions:**
- **8 January** — Bowie would have turned 80; the official estate already announced Bowie80 celebrations.
- **14 January** — the UK release of *Low* turns 50, verified by the official Bowie site.

**Reuse first:** The existing four Bowie articles offer album reset, duration, album shape and representative tracks. These may be enough for a new LinkedIn sequence without a new dataset.

**Possible *new* synthesis:** Build a story around *Low*'s album shape: what changes if you plot every original-edition track rather than summarise the album with a mean or one hit?

**Three-chart *hypotheses to test***:
1. *Low* against adjacent verified studio albums: full track-duration distributions.
2. Original album-side differences, if side and track order can be verified and kept consistent.
3. An established style-reset score shown alongside the actual tracks to illustrate the limitations of aggregate labels, not to judge artistic quality.

**Data route:** Existing Bowie project files under `archive/projects`; inspect exact definitions, album inclusions and provenance **before** refreshing. Official Bowie track listings and release-date notes. No fresh rights or data invented.

**Blockbuster pairing:** Bowie has film credits, but a birthday-specific cinematic quote needs normal speaker/quote verification. If none passes, use the standalone music/article anniversary connection with a modest social CTA.

**Editorial recommendation:** This is the **best January anchor** because it connects an unusually good pair of milestone dates with four already researched posts. **Priority A; hybrid.**

## Working editorial rule

**An anniversary is the invitation, not the conclusion.** If data does not produce a compelling finding, reuse an older article, publish a single strong chart, or skip. Nothing is owed to the calendar.

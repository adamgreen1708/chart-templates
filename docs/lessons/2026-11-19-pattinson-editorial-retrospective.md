# Case review: Robert Pattinson → Bond (19 November 2026)

**Purpose:** honest retrospective of the editorial failures, what corrected them and how to prevent the user doing the analyst's job. These are **process lessons**, not a claim that the first or final charts are universally correct.

## What failed initially

The first three v1 visualisations were polished but shallow:

| First chart | Why it underperformed | Stronger replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Three broad career-period IMDb rating medians | Arbitrary bins smoothed away the films, chronology, franchise attention and any genuine tension. Three numbers are not automatically a discovery. | Plot all five Twilight titles and compare them explicitly with the other rated-film group. Show the franchise's share of title-votes as a separate neutral statistic. |
| Generic IMDb rating-vote scatter | It showed a relationship without a clear finding, and called out films by celebrity/editorial interest rather than a consistent visible selection criterion. | Show all 33 film credits over time with source-sized vote circles, and **label by transparent rules:** highest IMDb rating, most votes overall and most Twilight votes. |
| Bond-actor-era counts | Counting reused film-pair edges disguised the six actual people, Bond characters and Pattinson films. The interesting cast network vanished. | Six explicit film → shared performer → Bond-character links, de-duplicated by person and film; the Pierce Brosnan *Remember Me* relationship becomes a genuine editorial reveal. |

**Root cause:** The old workflow required “story discovery” but did not demand **competing hypotheses, a meaningful comparator, evidence per label, explicit alternative chart forms or a rejection decision**. It optimised for producing three neat images, not three discoveries.

## Corrections the user had to request

- Replace a slanted “None above 5.6” chart headline with a neutral descriptive statement. Better: use **mean 5.2**, not **median 5.1**, when we actually mean “average film score”; calculate the other group using the *same aggregation* (mean **6.6**, rather than retaining median 6.65).
- A blue **24.8%** vote-share number appeared to represent the blue “other films” mark: keep contextual headline statistics neutral; reserve red and blue for their actual groups and match marker/label colours.
- The scale used to place dots lacked tick labels: display the **4–9** axis explicitly and say when an axis is truncated.
- The chart 2 sentence about a mathematical **Pareto frontier** was clipped and too technical. It was true under the measured two-variable definition, but not the most useful reader takeaway. Remove the rings and explain **“Bigger circle = more IMDb votes”**.
- Annotations `Good Time` and `The Lighthouse` were meaningful films in a narrative sense, but did not fit the same rule as the statistical leaders. If an annotation is editorially selected, identify it as an example, not a ranking; otherwise apply a transparent rule.
- The original film-to-Bond overlap was labelled as actor-era counts; 14 film-pair edges were not 14 distinct people. De-duplicate explicitly and retain role-source provenance.
- The first feature illustration was rejected as visually cluttered; the accepted story-specific orbital/tuxedo composition was a creative **non-data image**, distinct from GitHub-rendered charts.
- **Absolute render rule clarified:** All charts, including early concepts with real values, must be generated by verified repository data and GitHub rendering—not ChatGPT image generation. PDF carousels can compose the exact approved image bytes but must not redraw data.

## What was better in the end

The final editorial sequence used three genuinely different reader discoveries:

1. **Paradox:** five Twilight films accrued approximately 24.8% of the *sum of title-votes* in this 33-film snapshot but had an unweighted arithmetic mean IMDb film rating of 5.2 versus 6.6 across 28 other films.
2. **Non-linear career:** a chronology of all 33 eligible titles, with vote count size and selected labels chosen by recorded criteria, not arbitrary name recognition.
3. **Human connection:** six verified shared-cast connections to Bond roles, with a former Bond having played Pattinson's father in *Remember Me* (2010).

These are observations **within a frozen, curated IMDb title-level research snapshot**, not actor-performance ratings, box-office popularity or proof that a colleague influenced casting. Source completeness and the distinctions between actor, title and cast-pair grain still need to be stated every time.

The blog headline and final image made the topic approachable without smuggling new chart facts into a poster.

## Concrete enduring changes

1. **No “three charts to fill three slots”.** Compare evidence-led routes before creating configs. Reject an entire first set when its finding is weak, even if the renderer and layout pass.
2. **Same grain and comparator** for a claim: population, time, numerator/denominator and aggregation recorded, then calculated in GitHub.
3. **Chart 3 should land something genuinely new**, rather than another numeric breakdown.
4. **Selection protocols** for every label, e.g. `max(votes)`, `max(rating)`, `max(votes where franchise=Twilight)`. Editorial examples require explicit alternative explanation and must not masquerade as statistical leaders.
5. **Plain, truthful graphic language** and axis disclosure; no unnecessary jargon, unlabeled shapes, misleading colour semantics, cropped explanatory prose or invisible units.
6. **GitHub-only render contract**, final-image SHA continuity, full/390/320-pixel inspection, and separate editorial/publication approval.
7. **No false promises of QA**: dimensional/pixel-edge checks are necessary but not sufficient. Manually inspect line-to-label pairing, caption/footer spacing and whether the visual tells a worthwhile story.
8. **Try a more inventive structure** where justified by the data: real networks deserve relationship diagrams; distributions need full ranges; time needs chronology rather than invented arbitrary eras. Do not introduce novelty for its own sake.
9. **Keep publishing controls separate:** the source/research draft PR can remain open even when the approved publication package merges; never claim unmerged research tooling has reached `main`.

## Analyst's self-review: what would have prevented the user feedback?

Before showing v1, one senior analyst should have asked:

- “Why are these three time periods meaningful rather than arbitrary?” (No sufficient answer.)
- “What do the rating and vote measures mean, and why does this scatter exist?” (Too vague.)
- “Why count Bond films when the real story is who Pattinson worked with?” (Wrong unit of analysis.)
- “Can a reader identify the actual source of each cast connection?” (No.)
- “Does the chosen label follow a replicable rule?” (Not initially.)
- “Would I be interested enough to share these three charts?” (Probably not.)

The correct response would have been to **redo discovery, not ask the user to improve mediocre layouts**.

## Post-publication review of the actual approved artefacts (19 November 2026)

**Review basis:** Published `site/_posts/2026-11-19-robert-pattinson-was-already-in-bonds-orbit.md` on `main`, plus the **actual approved 1600px PNGs**, whose SHA-256 values match publication files. This is a *post-mortem*, not a mandate to modify what Adam has already approved.

**Chart 1 — The five Twilight films averaged 5.2 on IMDb.**

What works: five individually labelled film-level marks on an explicit 4–9 scale; like-for-like unweighted **mean 5.2 versus 6.6**; group colour correspondence and neutral `24.8%` contextual statistic. This is a defensible contextual comparison rather than a generic franchise opinion.

What could still be stronger next time: the film-by-film scores all sit near 5, but the other 28 films are represented by **one mean triangle only**, masking their spread. A reader cannot see how much the distributions overlap. The `24.8%` is *share of summed title-votes in an editorially selected 33-film cohort*; it is **not market share, share of viewers, or popularity measured over equal release windows**. If studying the underlying question further, test an all-33-title distribution or dot-strip with direct group overlap, and examine vote-age/film-role effects before using that statistic as a public hook.

**Chart 2 — The reinvention was not a straight line.**

What works: all 33 rated credits are plotted by year; circle-size legend is now in plain language; three explicit, reproducibly ranked annotations and leader lines identify The Batman (most title-votes), The Odyssey (highest rating) and Twilight (most Twilight title-votes). Confusing frontier rings and unexplained film annotations were removed.

Remaining editorial limitation: the title promises a **career reinvention**, but the plot actually shows scattered *film ratings and voting activity across time*. It has no direct measure of career strategy, role prominence, genre changes, status, industry perception or creative intent. The title is interpretative and defensible only as an opinion, not as a measured result. Those three “highest” labels follow a rule, but **maxima are not automatically insights**. For the next story, test a chronology split by **verified production role, genre, franchise status or critic reception** (if sourced), or a clearly described group/range comparison across eras *with genuinely justified boundaries*. Do not fabricate role-importance categories to rescue a narrative; test whether relevant data exist first. At mobile size, also check how close the size key is to footer credits.

**Chart 3 — He'd met Bond. And his villains.**

What works: six **named** Pattinson film → shared performer → Bond character/film connections, with source dates; de-duplicated people rather than repeated Bond-film edges. The red Brosnan row plus the `Remember Me` father-role disclosure yields a memorable human payoff. It is the strongest of these three as a story visual, not necessarily the most complex.

Remaining caveat: the six are *verified examples*, not the exhaustive co-star universe. The simple horizontal arrows denote a **shared-person relationship**, not chronology, a route of influence or causation; some Bond roles occurred before the Pattinson shared film, others afterwards. A future analogue should document arrow meaning clearly and check complete-cast coverage before claiming all actors or all relationships.

**Article — Robert Pattinson was already in Bond's orbit.**

What works: compact, conversational sequence; useful UK-English rhythm; chart 3 delivers an authentic full-circle ending; source note appropriately distinguishes films from performances and title-votes from unique viewers. This is much better than the first three charts would have supported.

Where editorial judgement can improve further without rewriting this approved post: the **strongest cinematic hook (Brosnan played his father)** arrives only in the third section. For future pieces, test opening with a glimpse of the best reveal, then earn it with the charts; do not give the entire punchline away. Reduce explanatory repetition between caption and article prose. Keep technical caveats close to the potential misunderstanding, rather than asking the bottom source note to rescue an overconfident claim.

### The behavioural correction

On a finished story, perform a second, sceptical **post-build editorial read** before requesting approval:

- Is the actual advertised finding present in the chosen axes and measured fields, or only inferred in the prose?
- Can the reader see a meaningful distribution or comparator, instead of a single group summary?
- Would the highlights still be worthwhile if they were not famous, and are rule-defined maxima the right annotations?
- Do network lines have an honest meaning (shared actor versus influence or chronology)?
- Have we considered the effects of sample selection, release windows, credit prominence and source-coverage gaps?
- Is the story's best human surprise sufficiently prominent early enough to earn attention?
- Would one stronger chart replace two adequate but repetitive charts?
- Would I *personally reject* a merely functional draft instead of sending it to Adam to rescue?

**Decision on today's published artefacts:** Leave all three approved PNGs and the published article **unchanged**. The goal is improving the *next* project, not retrospectively invalidating an approved and defensible piece. Record the shortcomings candidly; no silent chart revisions.

## Follow-up responsibility

This standard is a guide and a machine-checkable preflight, not an assertion of human-like expertise. Each new project needs a real editorial decision, source checks, and pixel inspection. The assistant should ask fewer unnecessary questions, do more independent checking, and offer better evidence-led judgements earlier.
